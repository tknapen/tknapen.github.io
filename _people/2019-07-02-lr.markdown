Luisa Raimondo was an MR physics PhD student at the Spinoza Centre working on line-scanning fMRI, which samples a single line through cortex at very high spatial and temporal resolution. She developed methods to improve the precision and signal-to-noise ratio of the scanned lines.

Her first paper implemented human line-scanning at 7T, sampling BOLD responses across cortical depth every 250 µm at a 200 ms repetition time ([Raimondo et al., 2021](/publications/2021-08-20-JCBFM/)). She then improved the method with multi-echo readouts, denoising and prospective motion correction ([Raimondo et al., 2022](/publications/2022-11-17-JNM/)), tested a spin-echo version of the sequence ([Raimondo et al., 2023](/publications/2023-01-10-MAGMA/)), and used line-scanning to compare the hemodynamic response across cortical depth in young and middle-aged adults ([Raimondo et al., 2025](/publications/2025-02-28-BT/)).
