Gilles de Hollander joined the lab in 2017 as a postdoctoral researcher funded by an NWO-CAS grant, a collaboration with the Beijing lab of Peng Zhang and Sheng He. After a master's in artificial intelligence, he did his PhD in Birte Forstmann's lab at the University of Amsterdam. There he combined cognitive modeling with 7T structural and functional MRI to study the functional anatomy of the subthalamic nucleus, a small node of the basal ganglia, and optimized fMRI protocols for that region.

In the lab he used laminar-resolved fMRI to study the neural substrate of binocular rivalry in primary visual cortex. This work separated feedforward and feedback activity within the layers of human ocular dominance columns ([de Hollander et al., 2021](/publications/2021-03-12-NI/)) and contributed to a study of how rivalry is resolved at the mesoscale ([Qian et al., 2025](/publications/2025-11-13-NHB/)).
