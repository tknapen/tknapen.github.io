Martin Szinte joined the lab in 2016 on a Marie Curie fellowship. He did his PhD in Patrick Cavanagh's lab in Paris, followed by an extended postdoctoral fellowship on a Humboldt grant in Heiner Deubel's lab in Munich. His expertise is in eye movements and their link to perception.

In Amsterdam he used 7T fMRI and MRI-derived forward models to study how the brain plans eye movements and keeps vision stable across them. With Tomas Knapen he showed that the default network deactivates as a function of the position of a visual stimulus, so that it encodes visual space by means of deactivation ([Szinte & Knapen, 2020](/publications/2020-05-18-CC/)).
