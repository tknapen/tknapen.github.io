Jurjen Heij was a neuroscience PhD student at the Spinoza Centre working on line-scanning fMRI. He developed the methods to plan the line so that it targets a small, preselected patch of cortex, and used pRF modeling and time-course estimation to answer his research questions.

His framework selects a target location from anatomical and functional data and places the line there in a later session ([Heij et al., 2023](/publications/2023-08-22-HBM/)). With it he showed that stimuli in the center and in the surround of a population receptive field evoke distinct response profiles across cortical depth in V1 ([Heij et al., 2025](/publications/2025-06-19-IS/)). He also used quantitative 7T MRI to study intracortical myelination in major depressive disorder ([Heij et al., 2024](/publications/2024-06-20-TP/)). His analysis tools are in the [linescanning](https://github.com/gjheij/linescanning) package.
